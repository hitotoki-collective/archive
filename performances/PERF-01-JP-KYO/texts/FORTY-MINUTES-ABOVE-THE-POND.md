# Forty Minutes Above the Pond

Nobody had met before. That is the first fact, and the strangest one: four
strangers walked into one of Kyoto's holy places carrying instruments and
brushes, and were asked to build something with no constraints at all.

Listen to what they describe and it is all weather. Wind weaving. A flute that
takes impressions straight from nature, arriving without knowing what sound it
wants. A guitar player thrown into the unknown and thriving there. A pianist
reaching parts of himself he had not met, while being conscious — acutely,
respectfully — of the hundreds of years standing behind the tea house.

Around them: birds, water, koi turning under the surface. Above them, if the
priests are right, a dragon floating over the pond, unseen and load-bearing.

The painter is fourth generation. He grew up near Arashiyama, went to
kindergarten in Saga, was collected by a grandfather who walked him home past
this very water. The grandfather is long gone. The grandson keeps returning. On
this day he finally performed here, and the flute player says the dragon came to
him as a gift — given in ink, by hand, while he played.

Nobody was following anybody. Music prompted the brush; the brush prompted the
music. The genres did not match and the language did not need to. One of them
lost the feeling for time and space entirely.

Forty minutes. Then it was over, and unrepeatable, which was always the point:
the absolute moment of the performance, captured rather than composed.
